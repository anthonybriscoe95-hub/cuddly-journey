# 💷 Pricing & Quoting (simple system + templates)

Don't overthink pricing. Use a **minimum charge + size adjustment**, add a **first-clean premium**, and price so you'd happily do that job all day. Replace [brackets] with your own numbers for your area.

---

## Step 1 — Set your minimum charge
The lowest you'll do **any** job for (covers stopping the van and your time).
- Example UK: **£10**. US/Canada/AUS one-off cleans are usually much higher.
- **Never go below it**, even for a tiny flat.

## Step 2 — Price by size
More/bigger windows = more money. A simple ladder:

| Property | Rough price (set your own) |
|---|---|
| Small — terrace/townhouse, few windows | Minimum charge |
| Medium — semi / 3-bed | Minimum + ~50% |
| Large — detached / 4-bed+ | ~2× minimum |
| Very large / lots of glass / awkward | 2×+ minimum, judge it |

> Quick method: count the windows, or just look at the house and compare it to ones you've already priced. You'll get fast at eyeballing it within a week.

## Step 3 — Add for extras
- **Conservatory / sunroom** — add to the price (roof = more).
- **Frames & sills** — usually included on the first clean; after that mention "frames and sills wiped" as part of the service.
- **Velux / skylights, awkward access, very high** — add for difficulty.
- **Interior windows** — separate price from the regular exterior round.

## Step 4 — First-clean premium
The first clean is always the dirtiest and slowest. Charge **1.5×–2× the regular price** for it, then the normal price every cycle after. Always tell the customer up front so there's no surprise.

## Step 5 — Frequency
Recurring beats one-off every time. Offer:
- **Every 4 weeks** (most popular, best for you) — your standard.
- **Every 8 weeks** — fine, slightly higher per clean (dirtier).
- **One-off** — charge the **first-clean premium** price (no repeat income, so make it worth it).

## Commercial pricing
- Price **per visit** based on time + frequency (weekly/fortnightly).
- Small per-visit price is fine if it's **regular and clustered** near other work.
- Factor in: number/size of panes, ground-floor vs high, how often.
- Mention you're **insured** — often required.

---

## ✅ The golden rule
**If you're fully booked, your prices are too low.** Keep your existing customers' prices and **quote new customers a bit higher** until your bookings ease off. Raising prices on *new* work (not existing customers) is the cleanest way to earn more without upsetting anyone.

> • **Then £[Y] every 4 weeks** — frames & sills included

> "For a house like yours it'd be **£[Y]** every 4 weeks, and the first clean is **£[X]** as it's the dirtiest. Frames and sills included, and I'll text you each time I've been. Shall I get you booked in?"
