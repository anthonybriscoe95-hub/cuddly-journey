# 🪟 The 90-Day Plan to Get Window Cleaning Customers

**For:** A solo/small window cleaner who needs customers **tomorrow**, **next week**, and over the **next 3 months**.

**The big idea:** You don't need a fancy website or a big budget. You need to *talk to people on the streets near you, every day,* get a few happy customers, and turn them into a **recurring round** that pays you the same money over and over. That's the whole game.

> **Struggling to find customers? Read this first.** Almost every window cleaner who "can't find customers" has the same problem: they're *waiting* for customers to come to them (a Facebook page, a website, an ad) instead of *going to get them* (knocking doors on the street, today). The fastest, cheapest, most reliable way to fill a window cleaning round is **door-knocking the streets near where you already are.** Everything else in this plan supports that. Don't skip it.

# Part A — Everything about the window cleaning business

## A1. How the money actually works
There are two kinds of window cleaning work, and the difference is *everything*:

| | **One-off cleans** | **Recurring "round" work** ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Someone wants their windows done once | Same customer, every 4–8 weeks, forever |
| Income | Unpredictable, always chasing new people | Predictable, builds up, pays you again and again |
| Effort to get | High (constant marketing) | Get them **once**, keep them for years |
| **The goal** | Avoid relying on these | **This is the real business** |

**The recurring round is the prize.** A round is just a list of regular customers grouped by street/area, cleaned on a repeating cycle (commonly every **4 weeks** in the UK, or **monthly / bi-monthly** elsewhere). Once a customer is on your round, you turn up, clean, get paid, and come back next cycle — no marketing needed. Build a round of 150–250 houses and you have a full-time income that's the same every month.

**Why density matters:** if 8 houses on one street are all yours, you park once and earn for an hour without driving. If your customers are scattered across town, you burn the day driving. **So the rule is: always try to sell the houses next to a customer you already have.**

### Rough price ranges (set your own using Part A3)
These are *ballpark* figures to orient you — your local market sets the real numbers, so ask around / check what local cleaners charge.

- **Typical UK terrace/semi house:** ~£10–£20 per clean (exterior, every 4 weeks)
- **Larger detached house:** ~£20–£40+
- **US/Canada/AUS residential:** often priced higher and less frequently (e.g. $100–$300+ for a full interior+exterior, a few times a year) — the "every-4-weeks round" model is strongest in the UK; elsewhere you'll lean more on quarterly/seasonal cleans plus commercial.
- **Commercial storefront:** small per-visit price (e.g. £5–£15 / $10–$30) but done **weekly or fortnightly** and clustered on one street = great steady money.
- **First clean costs more:** the first time is always dirtier and slower — charge a **first-clean premium** (e.g. 1.5×–2× the regular price), then the recurring price after.

## A2. Equipment — start cheap, upgrade later
You do **not** need to spend thousands to start. Two methods:

**1. Traditional method (cheapest to start — get going this week):**
- Squeegee (a good one, e.g. 10"/12") + spare rubbers
- T-bar / applicator (washer) + a microfibre or scrim cloth for edges
- Bucket + window cleaning soap (a few drops of washing-up liquid works to start)
- Ladder (for upstairs) + ladder standoff/stay
- Detailing cloths/scrim for drips and frames
- A few microfibre cloths, a scraper for paint/muck
- 👉 You can start traditional for **roughly £50–£150 / $70–$200.**

**2. Water-fed pole (WFP) system (upgrade — buy when you have income):**
- A telescopic pole + brush + purified (de-ionised) water lets you clean upstairs windows **from the ground** (safer, faster, no ladders).
- Needs purified water (DI resin vessel or RO filter) + a tank/backpack.
- Costs more upfront (a starter backpack setup can be a few hundred; a tank/van system more) but it's faster, safer, and lets you clean high windows — most cleaners move to WFP once the round is paying.
- 👉 **Start traditional, reinvest your first weeks' earnings into a WFP setup.**

**Also (don't skip):**
- **Public liability insurance** — protects you if you damage a window or property. Often cheap for a sole trader and frequently *required* before commercial clients will hire you. ✅ **Check your local requirements** (rules differ by country) and get covered before you start charging.
- **Register as self-employed / sole trader** for tax as your country requires (e.g. HMRC in the UK; report self-employment income wherever you are). Keep it simple, just keep records (Part E).

## A3. Pricing — a simple way to never under-charge
Don't agonise. Use a floor + a size adjustment:

1. **Set a minimum charge.** No job is worth doing for less than this (e.g. £10 / $40). Even a tiny flat, you don't go below the minimum — it's not worth stopping the van.
2. **Price by size / number of windows.** More/bigger windows = more money. A quick rule many cleaners use:
   - Small (terrace/townhouse, few windows): minimum charge
   - Medium (semi/3-bed): minimum + ~50%
   - Large (detached/4-bed+): roughly 2× the minimum, more for big or awkward properties
3. **Add for extras:** conservatory roof, frames + sills (often included in first clean, then mention "frames and sills done"), Velux/skylights, hard-to-reach, lots of glass.
4. **First clean premium:** 1.5×–2× regular price (it's always the dirtiest, slowest one).
5. **Commercial:** price per visit based on time + frequency. A shopfront done weekly is small per visit but adds up and is super efficient if it's near other work.

**Golden rule:** price so you'd be happy to do that job all day. If you're booked solid, your prices are too low — raise them on new customers. (Quote template is in [`templates/pricing-and-quote-sheet.md`](./templates/pricing-and-quote-sheet.md).)

## A4. Where customers come from (ranked by SPEED to first customer)
Do them roughly in this order:

1. **People you already know** ⚡ *(customers today)* — friends, family, neighbours, ex-colleagues. A personal message + one social post gets your first job fastest.
2. **Door-knocking the streets near you** ⚡⚡ *(customers today/this week)* — the single most effective method. See [`templates/door-knock-script.md`](./templates/door-knock-script.md).
3. **Free local listings** — Google Business Profile (huge — see [`templates/google-business-profile-setup.md`](./templates/google-business-profile-setup.md)), plus free local directories.
4. **Reviews & referrals** — every happy customer is a source of 2–3 more. Always ask. See [`templates/review-request-message.md`](./templates/review-request-message.md).
5. **Local Facebook groups & Nextdoor** *(free, fast)* — community "recommend a window cleaner" posts. See [`templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md`](./templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md).
6. **Flyers / door hangers / business cards** *(cheap)* — especially the "Sorry I missed you" hanger when no one answers the door. See [`templates/flyer-text.md`](./templates/flyer-text.md).
7. **Paid ads (Facebook/Google Local)** *(only when budget allows)* — useful later; don't rely on it early.
8. **Leaflet drops at scale** — good for filling gaps in streets you already work.

> 💡 The cheap/free top of this list (1, 2, 4, 5) is where you'll get **almost all** of your first 90 days of customers. Paid stuff is optional.

## A5. Keeping customers (this is where the business is actually won)
Getting a customer is hard; keeping one is easy *if you do these:*
- **Turn up reliably and on schedule.** The #1 complaint about window cleaners is "mine stopped showing up." Just being reliable beats most competitors.
- **Clean to a consistent standard** — no streaks, do the frames/sills, leave it tidy.
- **Tell them you've been:** a quick text "Hi, cleaned your windows today, £X, thanks!" with payment details. Makes paying easy and reminds them you're dependable.
- **Cluster your round** (density) so you can offer a good price and never miss a clean.
- **Ask for reviews and referrals** at the happy moment (right after a great clean).
- **Make it easy to pay** — cash, bank transfer, or a simple card/app link. The easier to pay, the faster you get paid and the longer they stay.

# Part B — TOMORROW (Day 1): get your first leads in 24 hours

**Today's goal: 5–10 real conversations and 1–3 booked quotes or cleans.** You can absolutely book your first job tomorrow. Here's the hour-by-hour:

### ☀️ Morning (1–2 hours) — the "warm" leads
1. **Post once on your personal social media** (Facebook/Instagram). Use the post in [`templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md`](./templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md). "I'm running a window cleaning business — first 5 local people who book this week get [their first clean at a discount / frames done free]. Know anyone? Please share!"
2. **Send 10 direct messages/texts** to people you know locally (template in the same file). Friends, family, neighbours. Ask them *and* ask them to pass your name on.
3. **Set up your free Google Business Profile** ([`templates/google-business-profile-setup.md`](./templates/google-business-profile-setup.md)). It can take a few days to verify, so **start it today** — it works for you 24/7 once live.

### 🚪 Midday/Afternoon (2–3 hours) — door-knocking (your money-maker)
4. **Pick 2–3 streets near your home** (or near anyone who's already said yes). Knock every door with the script in [`templates/door-knock-script.md`](./templates/door-knock-script.md).
5. **Where no one answers, leave a "Sorry I missed you" door hanger/card** (text in [`templates/flyer-text.md`](./templates/flyer-text.md)) with your number. A surprising number call back.
6. **Write down every "yes" and "maybe"** with the address and price you quoted (use the tracker in Part E). Book a clean time on the spot if you can.

### 🌙 Evening (30 mins) — community groups
7. **Post in 2–3 local Facebook groups / Nextdoor** ("[Town] Community", "[Town] Recommendations", local village/estate groups). Use the community post in [`templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md`](./templates/facebook-and-nextdoor-posts.md). Reply to anyone asking for a window cleaner.

✅ **End of Day 1 you should have:** several conversations, 1–3 jobs booked or quoted, your Google listing started, and a list of doors to revisit. That's a real start.

# Part C — NEXT WEEK (Days 2–7): build momentum

**Week 1 goal: 10–20 quotes given, 5–10 paying customers, your first reviews live.**

Do a version of Day 1 **every day**, plus build the foundations:

### Every day this week
- **Knock doors for 2+ hours**, focusing on **streets near any job you've booked** (density!). Every booked job → knock the 10 houses either side and across the road: *"Hi, I'm just starting to clean a few windows on this street — would you like yours done too?"* People say yes far more when neighbours are already customers.
- **Do the jobs you booked**, do them brilliantly, and **text the customer after** ("cleaned today, £X, thank you — see you in 4 weeks").
- **Ask every happy customer two things:** (1) a quick online review, (2) "do you know anyone else who'd like theirs done?" Offer a small **referral reward** (e.g. their next clean half price for a referral that signs up). Script in [`templates/review-request-message.md`](./templates/review-request-message.md).

### One-time setup tasks this week
- **Finish your Google Business Profile** and add **before/after photos** of your first jobs.
- **Create a simple free Facebook business Page** (name, area, phone, photos) so you look legit.
- **Order cheap business cards + flyers** (online print shops are very cheap) and **design a "Sorry I missed you" door hanger** — these turn no-answer doors into callbacks. Wording in [`templates/flyer-text.md`](./templates/flyer-text.md).
- **Open the simplest possible way to get paid** (bank transfer details ready to text; consider a free card/payment app).

✅ **End of Week 1 you should have:** your first handful of recurring customers, a couple of reviews, photos, and a daily knocking habit forming. Momentum is everything now — **keep knocking.**

# Part D — THE 90 DAYS (month by month)

Three months, three jobs: **(1) get your first customers, (2) build density & referrals, (3) turn it into a repeatable round.** Numbers below are realistic targets for a solo cleaner working at it consistently — adjust to your hours and area.

## 📅 Month 1 — Foundation & first customers
**Goal:** Build the daily habit, get listed everywhere free, land your first **10–20 recurring customers** and **5+ reviews**, and settle on your pricing.

**Weekly actions:**
- [ ] Knock doors **at least 3–5 days/week**, always near existing customers.
- [ ] Do every job to a high standard; **text-after** every clean.
- [ ] Ask for a **review + referral** after every happy job.
- [ ] Add each new customer to your **round tracker** with their next-clean date (Part E).
- [ ] Finish/optimise **Google Business Profile**; post before/after photos.
- [ ] Post weekly in **local Facebook groups / Nextdoor**.

**Month-1 targets:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Quotes given | 40–80 |
| Recurring customers signed | 10–20 |
| Reviews live | 5+ |
| First-clean income | Reinvest some into WFP/insurance/cards |

## 📅 Month 2 — Density & referrals
**Goal:** Make every new customer *near an existing one*, switch on referrals, and start a few commercial leads. Target **40–60 recurring customers**.

**Weekly actions:**
- [ ] Focus all canvassing on **streets you already serve** — fill them out (aim for clusters of 5–10 houses per street).
- [ ] Launch a **referral scheme** ("£5 off / free clean for every neighbour who signs up") — tell every customer.
- [ ] **Leaflet-drop the streets you're cleaning that day** ("I'm cleaning windows on your street today — want to join the round?").
- [ ] **Walk into 3–5 local shops/cafés/offices** and offer a regular storefront clean (commercial = steady money; clustering them on one street/parade is ideal). Use the commercial bit of [`templates/door-knock-script.md`](./templates/door-knock-script.md).
- [ ] If budget allows, trial a **small local Facebook/Google ad** (£20–£50 / $30–$60) targeting your town.
- [ ] Keep collecting reviews — aim for a steady trickle.

**Month-2 targets:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Recurring customers (total) | 40–60 |
| Avg. customers per active street | 5+ |
| Commercial leads started | 2–5 |
| Referrals received | A handful/week |

## 📅 Month 3 — Systemise & scale
**Goal:** Turn your customers into a smooth **repeating round** with steady weekly income, and decide how to grow. Target **a near-full round** for your hours.

**Weekly actions:**
- [ ] Organise customers into a **4-weekly (or monthly) cycle by area** so each working day is one zone — minimal driving.
- [ ] Send **reminder texts** the day before / morning of each clean (reduces locked gates & no-access).
- [ ] **Raise your prices on new customers** if you're consistently fully booked (you probably are if this is working).
- [ ] **Pitch a few proper commercial contracts** (offices, estate agents, small chains) for weekly/fortnightly work.
- [ ] Decide your next investment: **water-fed pole system**, a **second person/helper**, or **van signage**.
- [ ] Review your numbers (below) and double down on what's bringing customers cheapest.

**Month-3 targets:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Recurring round | Near-full for your hours (often 80–150+ houses solo, area-dependent) |
| Income | Steady, predictable weekly revenue |
| Schedule | Organised into repeating zones |
| Next step chosen | WFP / helper / commercial contracts |

## 📊 What to track every week (all 3 months)
Keep these five numbers — they tell you exactly what's working:
1. **Quotes/conversations given**
2. **Conversion %** (customers ÷ quotes)
3. **New recurring customers**
4. **Reviews collected**
5. **Weekly revenue**

If conversion is low → improve your door pitch/price. If quotes are low → knock more doors. Simple.

# Part E — Tools, tracking & money

## E1. Your round tracker (don't skip — this *is* your business asset)
A window cleaning round is literally a **list of customers and when they're due.** Keep it in a free spreadsheet (Google Sheets) or a cheap window-cleaner app. Minimum columns:

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Name | Jane Smith |
| Address | 12 Oak Street |
| Phone | 07… |
| Price | £15 |
| Frequency | Every 4 weeks |
| Last cleaned | 2026-06-28 |
| Next due | 2026-07-26 |
| Notes | Side gate code 1234, dog, pay by transfer |
| Paid? | ✅ / owes £15 |

> Sort by **street/area** and **next-due date** so each working day = one zone with no missed cleans. As the round grows, a dedicated window-cleaning app (several cheap ones exist) can automate scheduling, reminder texts, and payments — but a spreadsheet is fine to start.

## E2. Money & admin (keep it simple)
- **Get paid easily:** have bank transfer details ready to text; cash is fine; a free card-reader/payment-app helps. Easier payment = you get paid faster and keep customers longer.
- **Records for tax:** note every payment in and every expense (equipment, fuel, insurance, cards). A single spreadsheet tab is enough to start. Report self-employment income as your country requires.
- **Insurance:** get **public liability** before charging — cheap, and many customers (especially commercial) require it. ✅ Check your local rules.
- **Register as self-employed/sole trader** per your country's rules.

## E3. The one habit that beats everything
**Knock doors every working day, near your existing customers, and turn up reliably.** Do that for 90 days and you'll have a round. Everything else in this plan just makes that easier.
